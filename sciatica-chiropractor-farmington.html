Sciatica information for Farmington, Michigan

Leg pain or sciatica symptoms in Farmington?

Pain that begins around the lower back or buttock and travels into a leg is often described as sciatica. Similar symptoms can have different causes, so first check for warning signs and then arrange an appropriate evaluation.

Understanding symptoms

What does sciatica usually feel like?

Sciatica describes symptoms associated with irritation or pressure involving the sciatic nerve. People often describe pain that starts in the lower back or buttock and travels down one leg. Burning, tingling, numbness, or weakness may occur, but the pattern can vary.

Leg pain is not automatically sciatica, and this page cannot diagnose the cause. An in-person history and examination can help distinguish among possible causes and determine whether referral or additional medical evaluation is needed.

Safety first

Know when back and leg symptoms need urgent care.

Seek urgent medical care if back or leg pain follows a serious injury, or comes with new bowel or bladder problems, trouble urinating, numbness around the groin, marked or increasing leg weakness, fever, unexplained weight loss, difficulty walking, or severe and rapidly worsening pain.

Do not wait for a chiropractic appointment in an emergency.

Call 911 or seek emergency medical care for sudden or severe symptoms, major trauma, new loss of bowel or bladder control, or other symptoms that make you think you may be having an emergency.

Prepare for an evaluation

What details should I track?

  • Where the symptoms begin and exactly how far they travel down the leg.
  • Whether the discomfort is aching, sharp, burning, tingling, numb, or associated with weakness.
  • Whether coughing, sitting, walking, bending, or another activity changes it.
  • When it began, whether there was an injury, and whether it is improving or worsening.
  • Your health history, medicines, recent procedures, and previous back or nerve problems.

One possible option

Can chiropractic care help sciatica symptoms?

Chiropractic care may be considered for some people with back-related leg symptoms, but it is not appropriate for every cause. Evidence for spinal manipulation and sciatica is more limited than the evidence for low back pain generally. No treatment can guarantee relief, and an accurate assessment matters before any care begins.

Tell the chiropractor your complete health history.

Share diagnoses, medicines, recent injuries, procedures, pregnancy status, and any numbness or weakness. Dr. Anderson may recommend medical evaluation or referral if symptoms appear outside the scope of chiropractic care.

The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health reports that spinal manipulation may provide small improvements for some people with low back pain. Benefits and risks depend on the person and condition, so discuss your circumstances with a qualified healthcare professional.
