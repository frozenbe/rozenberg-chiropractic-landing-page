Back pain information for Farmington, Michigan

My back hurts in Farmington—what should I do?

Back pain can have many causes. Start by checking for warning signs, then gather a few details about your symptoms. If the situation is not urgent, a conversation with a qualified health professional can help you decide on an appropriate next step.

Step one

Check whether your back pain needs urgent medical attention.

Most back pain is not an emergency, but some symptoms should be assessed promptly. Seek urgent medical care if back pain follows a serious fall or injury, or comes with new bowel or bladder problems, trouble urinating, numbness around the groin, significant leg weakness or numbness, fever, unexplained weight loss, difficulty walking, or pain that is severe, constant, or rapidly worsening.

Do not wait for a chiropractic appointment in an emergency.

Call 911 or seek emergency medical care for sudden or severe symptoms, major trauma, new loss of bowel or bladder control, or other symptoms that make you think you may be having an emergency.

This page provides general information and cannot determine the cause of your pain.

Step two

If it is not an emergency, note what you are feeling.

A clear description can make a phone call or appointment more useful. Consider writing down:

  • When the pain started and whether an injury or unusual activity came first.
  • Where it hurts and whether pain travels into a buttock or leg.
  • Whether you have tingling, numbness, weakness, or changes in balance.
  • Which movements, positions, or times of day make it better or worse.
  • Your health conditions, recent procedures, medicines, and other care you have tried.

One possible option

Could chiropractic care be appropriate for low back pain?

Chiropractic care is one of several non-drug approaches people may consider for some types of low back pain. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health reports that spinal manipulation may lead to small improvements in pain and function for some people with acute or chronic low back pain. Results vary, and it is not appropriate for every person or every cause of pain.

An evaluation comes before treatment.

A chiropractor should take a health history, ask about your symptoms, and perform an appropriate examination. Share all relevant diagnoses, medicines, recent injuries, procedures, and pregnancy status. Dr. Anderson may recommend that you see another healthcare professional when symptoms appear outside the scope of chiropractic care.

No website can promise a diagnosis, cure, or particular result. Call the office if you are unsure whether scheduling a chiropractic evaluation makes sense for your situation.
